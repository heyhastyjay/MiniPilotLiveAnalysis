Carbon balance

Carbon in is the inlet flow times the feed carbon. Carbon out is the product-gas mass flow times the GC carbon (CO + CO2 + CH4). Both are sccm of carbon. The gap between the lines is what conversion treats as reacted carbon. Shaded bands are where that gap has to be corrected before it is called conversion.

StepWhat the program does
1 Read every *.LOG file in the SpecView folder. Channel names map to these log header names:
  • Reactor = Loop1.Process Value
  • reactor pressure = Loop4.Process Value
  • inlet flow = AIs-.Module5 Inp2
2 Read livelog.csv in the SpecView folder. Channel names map to these log header names:
  • L1PV = Reactor
  • L4PV = reactor pressure
  • Flow1 = inlet flow
3 Read every Cole-Parmer file matching flowlog-*.log. The line that starts with =~ and contains “log ” sets the clock, including the date. Column headers in order: pressure, temperature, volumetric flow, mass flow.
4 Read every datafile_*.rst in the TotalChrom folder, in file-number order. The injection time is the acquisition time (UTC) shifted 5 hours to show the time in EST. Peak area is the largest value between 100 and 1e9 found for Hydrogen, Carbon Monoxide, Carbon Dioxide, and Methane.
5 For each injection, collect Cole-Parmer seconds from 30 seconds before to 30 seconds after. With at least 10 samples, pressure, temperature, volumetric flow, and mass flow are the median. Otherwise those four cells stay blank.
6 For each injection, take the nearest SpecView sample. If it is more than 30 seconds away, temperature, pressure, and inlet flow stay blank. Inlet flow is also blank when it is negative or 9000 or higher.
7 The dead volume from the end of the reactor to the GC detector is 1 L. The gas passes the back pressure regulator and the Cole-Parmer meter on the way. Transit time (min) = 1 / product-gas volumetric flow (LPM). Gas left reactor is the injection time minus that transit time, rounded to the nearest second. Reactor temperature, pressure, and inlet flow at exit are the nearest SpecView sample to that time, with the same blank rules as step 6. A volumetric flow that is blank or 0 leaves these cells blank. The raw columns stay at the injection time.
8 Apply the curve from Calibration / inputs. Gas % = (peak area − intercept) / slope. These are the adjusted gas percents. They are not scaled to 100%. A gas with no slope is written as its peak area, and that column is labeled area instead of mol%. A missing peak stays blank and counts as 0 in the carbon sum.
9 Carbon out (mol%) is CO + CO2 + CH4. Feed carbon is the CO + CO2 + CH4 typed on Calibration / inputs, divided by 100. Inlet flow is the inlet at exit, in SLPM, and inlet K is 1. Outlet flow is the product-gas mass flow at the injection, in SLPM. Carbon conversion (%) = 100 × (inlet flow × feed carbon − outlet flow × carbon out / 100) / (inlet flow × feed carbon). No leak is subtracted. A blank inlet at exit, a blank mass flow, or a feed with no carbon leaves the conversion blank.
10 Write one CSV row per injection to the injection table file. Download table sends that same file. Analysis draws each conversion against reactor temperature at exit. Color is the inlet flow at exit. The table under that graph lists temperature, pressure, inlet flow, and carbon conversion for each of those injections.
